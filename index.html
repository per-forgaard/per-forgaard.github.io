01

The Problem: We Had No Idea What Was Actually Working

At Strim (a D2C streaming platform), we were spending across TV, social, search, display, and programmatic — but couldn't answer a basic question: what is the real effect of our media spend on short-term sales?

We faced three specific challenges that made this nearly impossible to answer with standard tools.

02

Attribution Was Broken — Every Platform Told a Different Story

Cookie lifespans had shrunk to 24 hours in most browsers, making cross-channel customer journeys invisible. "Direct" traffic in Google Analytics kept growing as attribution windows collapsed.

Worse, the platforms wildly disagreed on conversions. In one month, Google Analytics reported 135 sales from Facebook — while Facebook claimed nearly 3,000. For Snapchat, the gap was even larger.

We also couldn't distinguish sales driven by accumulated brand equity, word-of-mouth, and seasonal momentum from those actually caused by paid media. When a viral football match drove sign-ups, the platforms happily took credit.

03

The Solution: An Automated, Always-On MMM Pipeline

Together with our agency partner Dentsu, we built a fully automated marketing mix modeling system. The architecture was straightforward but the automation was key — traditional MMM takes months and delivers a static report. We needed something we could use weekly.

All data collection was automated via Supermetrics (digital) and direct feeds (offline, sales). BigQuery normalized everything. Python ran the modeling. Looker visualized the output into a weekly decision-making dashboard, not a quarterly report.

04

Key Finding: Only 20% of Sales Were Driven by Paid Media

The model revealed that approximately 80% of trial sign-ups were not caused by paid media — they were driven by accumulated brand awareness, word-of-mouth, product quality, and seasonal factors. Paid channels were responsible for about 20% of actual conversions.

This wasn't alarming — it's consistent with international benchmarks. But it meant our true cost per acquisition was 2–10x higher than what platforms reported. We introduced "iCPO" (incremental CPO) as our north star metric.

"It's not fun to tell stakeholders that paid search has an iCPO of $3,700 instead of $350 — but it's far more honest, and it's what actually lets you optimize."

05

The Result: 20% Reduction in Cost Per New Customer

By reallocating budget toward channels with higher incremental efficiency and away from those riding organic momentum and brand equity, we reversed a rising CPO trend and achieved a 20% reduction year-over-year.

Marketing & Customer Acquisition

When I started, the digital lead process was a black box. We had no visibility into how many leads submitted via the website actually qualified for Wealth Management — let alone how many signed as new clients. Through implementation of a lead management tool and rigorous follow-up at the advisor level, we built a full pipeline view: form submitted → advisor mapping → prospect meeting → client signed. This opened an entirely new landscape of analysis and let us quantify the true value of the digital channel.

On the media side, the team had historically relied on upper-funnel brand campaigns — print ads and high-visibility display in finance newspapers. I made it a priority in 2024 to introduce mid- and lower-funnel activities: LinkedIn, programmatic display, Meta, and paid search, all in a test-and-learn framework to scale what worked. By 2025, we were running campaigns across the full marketing funnel — from brand/upper funnel (airports, out-of-home, print, TV sponsorships) through mid-funnel (content marketing in digital newspapers, showcasing the breadth of our value proposition) to lower funnel (tactical ads across programmatic, paid social, search). The increased investment and leadership support only happened because we could demonstrate results at every stage.

Customer Value Proposition — "Private Banking 2.0"

Before ramping up campaigns at full speed, we ran a project to sharpen our Customer Value Proposition. This involved interviewing both existing clients and potential HNW prospects who didn't yet have a wealth manager — identifying pain points, perceptions, and barriers to entry. The findings confirmed our hypotheses: the personal relationship with the advisor was the most valued aspect, while the category was broadly perceived as expensive and exclusive to a very narrow group.

Together with our internal marketing function and agency, we built an entirely new CVP — a toolbox of refreshed imagery and messaging tailored per market, branded internally as "Private Banking 2.0" to signal forward momentum.

Enterprise CRM Migration

We had a legacy CRM system (Apsis) that was non-compliant and disconnected from customer data — meaning communications weren't personalized and outreach wasn't coordinated. I was responsible for migrating Wealth Management onto the Adobe stack, including Adobe Journey Optimizer for enterprise CRM. We identified prioritized digital customer journeys, mapped the necessary data requirements, and navigated a complex compliance and risk environment to migrate to AJO without any downtime. The result: one unified CRM system for all high-net-worth client communication across in-app (mobile and netbank), push, and email — a significantly improved customer experience.

Results

Back-to-back recognition in the Prospera survey (the most respected ranking in Nordic Wealth Management) as "The Strongest Private Banking Brand" in 2024 and 2025 — signaling the impact of our new CVP and increased marketing efforts. Additionally, improved digital campaigns and full-funnel analysis showed an increase in new clients from digital channels of +60% (Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025).
